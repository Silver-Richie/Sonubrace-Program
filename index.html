Sonubrace is a wrist-worn Doppler ultrasound band and this is its platform. It turns the echoes bouncing off your blood cells into three things you can read: how fast the blood is moving, which way it is going, and whether it is flowing smoothly — then explains what the picture means in ordinary words.

The three parameters Sonubrace measures

Everything else on this platform is derived from these three. They were chosen because together they describe a circulation completely enough to spot the haemodynamic changes that sit behind most non-communicable diseases.

Parameter 1

Velocity

How fast the blood moves, in metres per second. Found by taking the strongest frequency in each slice of the spectrogram and converting it with the Doppler equation. Peak systolic and end-diastolic velocity come straight from this.

Parameter 2

Direction

Which way the blood is travelling past the probe. A positive Doppler shift means toward it, negative means away. Splitting the echo into in-phase and quadrature channels is what preserves that sign instead of collapsing it to a magnitude.

Parameter 3

Pattern

Whether the flow is smooth, pulsatile, turbulent or irregular. Built from three measurements: how regularly the waveform repeats, how widely the velocities are spread, and the shape of the waveform itself.

Supporting indices

These are computed from the three parameters above, not measured separately. They are the numbers a clinician will recognise.

PSV

Peak systolic velocity — the fastest point of each beat.

EDV

End-diastolic velocity — flow just before the next beat.

RI

(PSV − EDV) / PSV — resistance downstream.

PI

(PSV − EDV) / mean — vessel elasticity and flow variability.

How a reading is produced

  1. Transmit. The microcontroller clocks out an ultrasound pulse, multiplies it by one segment of a truncated long code, mixes it with the carrier, and the DAC hands it to the transducer through the transmit/receive switch.
  2. Receive. The echo comes back carrying the Doppler shift. It is amplified, time-gain compensated, band-pass filtered and digitised.
  3. Demodulate and decode. The digitised echo goes to two mixers a quarter cycle apart — in-phase and quadrature — then through a matched filter against the same code segment that was transmitted. What comes out is the Doppler signal, with its sign intact.
  4. Transform. A short-time Fourier transform turns that signal into P(f, t), the Doppler spectrogram: what frequencies were present at each moment.
  5. Measure. The envelope is traced across the spectrogram, the cardiac cycles are segmented, and the three parameters plus the supporting indices are computed.
  6. Explain. The analyst combines those numbers with your health profile to describe what the picture shows and flag anything worth a clinician's attention.

What you get on the platform

Live monitor

Connect the band over Bluetooth, or run the simulator. The spectrogram, the velocity envelope, all three parameters and the supporting indices update as you record.

IMU angle guidance

A live illustration of your forearm, the band and the beam, showing your angle against the target and telling you when to hold still — because angle error distorts every velocity number.

Named recordings

Save each calibration session under a name you choose, rename it whenever you like, compare sessions over time and export everything as JSON for a clinician.

An analyst that answers back

A plain-language walkthrough of your spectrogram, NCD risk flags with the reasoning shown, risk-management suggestions, and a chat that answers questions about your own recording.

What Sonubrace is not

A screening aid, not a diagnosis

Sonubrace measures haemodynamics and highlights patterns. It cannot diagnose hypertension, diabetes, heart disease or anything else, and it is not a substitute for clinical assessment. Its risk score is a priority for review, not a probability of illness.

If you have chest pain, sudden weakness, difficulty speaking, or a limb that becomes cold, pale or painful, seek emergency care immediately rather than taking a reading.
